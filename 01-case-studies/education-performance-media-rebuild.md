# Case Study: Education Performance Media Rebuild

## Context

A complex, multi-institution education portfolio needed a more consistent performance media operating model across acquisition, measurement, agency execution, and executive reporting.

The issue was not a lack of campaign activity. The portfolio had too many differences in account structure, pacing, funnel visibility, and reporting practice for leaders to compare performance cleanly or intervene early when operating signals weakened.

## Challenge

The diagnostic finding was structural: media execution, application-stage measurement, and portfolio governance were not working from one shared operating standard.

That made it harder to distinguish a media problem from a measurement, funnel, market, or execution problem. Cost-per-enrollment trends and approved-application quality could be obscured by inconsistent account architecture, uneven pacing, and reporting that described activity without producing a clear decision.

## Approach

Led a portfolio-level rebuild across account architecture, measurement discipline, agency governance, operating cadence, and reporting.

Key moves included:

- Established clearer account and campaign structures so performance could be reviewed consistently across institutions
- Aligned optimization and reporting more closely to application-stage and cost-per-enrollment signals
- Introduced portfolio-level pacing reviews to identify budget risk and reallocation needs earlier
- Strengthened agency governance through shared expectations, issue escalation, and clearer ownership
- Created a recurring operating cadence that connected media, funnel, and measurement findings to decisions
- Reframed reporting around exceptions, tradeoffs, owners, and next actions instead of activity summaries

The rebuild created a common operating language. Media results could be interpreted against funnel quality and enrollment efficiency, while pacing and execution problems became easier to isolate before they spread across the portfolio.

## Outcome

The rebuild reduced cost-per-enrollment, improved approved-application efficiency, and stabilized budget pacing across the portfolio.

These are directional operating signals, not claims of revenue, margin, profit, or enterprise-value impact. The bounded result is that a more consistent media and governance system made application-stage performance easier to compare, manage, and improve.

## Jared's role

Led the performance media operating model across portfolio structure, measurement alignment, agency governance, pacing discipline, and executive decision support. The work required coordinating across institutions and partners while keeping optimization tied to application-stage and cost-per-enrollment signals.

## Reusable lesson

Portfolio performance cannot be governed through isolated account reviews. A shared architecture, measurement standard, and decision cadence are required before leaders can compare results, move budget confidently, or hold execution partners accountable.
