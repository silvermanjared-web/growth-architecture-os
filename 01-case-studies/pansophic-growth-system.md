# Case Study: Multi-Brand Education Growth System

## Context

A $700M PE-backed education enterprise with 180+ institutions needed performance media, CRO, measurement, agency work, and investment decisions to operate as one system.

The current mandate spans $15M+ in media across a complex portfolio and three business units. The growth challenge was not just channel performance. It was system performance.

## Problem

The environment had many moving parts:

- Multiple business units, institutions, agencies, and vendors
- Paid search, paid social, Performance Max, programmatic, YouTube, CTV, and brand media
- Enrollment-operations and web-development dependencies
- CRO and measurement dependencies
- Seasonal demand cycles and forecast pressure
- Executive visibility and allocation decisions

In that kind of environment, each team can optimize its own surface while the portfolio still lacks a shared view of spend, pacing, funnel quality, signal integrity, and ownership.

## Diagnosis

The operating model needed stronger connective tissue across five layers:

1. Media allocation logic
2. Measurement and conversion standards
3. Agency accountability
4. CRO and landing-path discipline
5. Executive decision cadence
## Operating moves

### Rebuild CRO as an operating model

Established five CRO workstreams with common standards for conversion logic, taxonomy, QA, pacing, reporting, escalation, and cross-functional ownership.

The model made experimentation and conversion work less ad hoc across media, analytics, web, and business stakeholders.

### Move capital when the portfolio required it

Redirected $500K in media investment across priority markets rather than protecting the existing plan when performance and forecast risk pointed elsewhere.

The point of the evidence is the allocation decision itself. It is not a claim of incremental revenue, profit, or savings.

### Correct channel mix against deeper funnel quality

Across a nine-school brick-and-mortar group, the acquisition mix had become too search-heavy. Following a broader channel-mix correction, lead-to-enrollment improved from approximately 17% to 28%, informing subsequent allocation planning.

The result is treated as an observed outcome after the broader mix change, not as proof of single-channel causality.

### Scale conversion and measurement infrastructure

Scaled acquisition and conversion infrastructure across 150+ custom landing pages while strengthening Google/Meta-to-Salesforce measurement, offline and deep-funnel conversion signals, and QA.

The objective was to make applications and enrollments increasingly usable as investment signals rather than optimizing only to lead volume.
### Stabilize execution at speed

Restructured 60+ campaigns in under four weeks during a high-pressure enrollment period, restoring clearer market, audience, and channel architecture while improving visibility into pacing and execution risk.

### Reset agency governance and decision rights

Tightened scope, pricing, budget controls, performance accountability, escalation, and in-housing decision rights across media, creative, analytics, and CRO.

The objective was not more vendor management. It was clearer accountability and earlier visibility into operating and spend risk.

## Resulting operating model

The portfolio moved toward a common decision system across:

- Investment allocation and pacing
- Campaign and audience architecture
- Conversion definitions and measurement QA
- CRO prioritization and rollout
- Agency scope and accountability
- Executive escalation and forecast risk

## Why this matters

The value is not simply better campaign management. It is better business control: leadership can see where demand is healthy, where signal is distorted, where investment should move, and which risks require a decision.

## Claim boundaries

The quantitative evidence in this case study is governed in `../00-positioning/claims/claims-registry.json`. The 17% to 28% result is observational after a broader mix correction. The $500K reallocation is an investment decision, not a claimed financial return. The 150+ landing pages and 60+ campaigns establish operating scale, not isolated causal outcomes.

## Reusable lesson

Complex growth portfolios need an operating model before they need more tactics. The system should make four things explicit: what we trust, who owns the decision, when risk escalates, and what should happen next.
