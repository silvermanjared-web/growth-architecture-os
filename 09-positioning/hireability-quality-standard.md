# Hireability Quality Standard

## Objective

Public positioning in this portfolio is optimized to make the strongest credible employer-relevant case for Jared Silverman.

**Accuracy is a hard constraint. Hireability is the positioning objective.**

Evidence governance determines what may be claimed. Positioning determines which supported truths matter most to a specific evaluator, how they should be sequenced, and how they translate into employer value.

The public narrative should not expose internal evidence-control language unless a boundary is materially necessary to understand the claim.

## Quality model

Employer-facing positioning is evaluated on a 100-point model:

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Truth & evidence | 20 |
| Role-requirement alignment | 15 |
| Intended communication | 10 |
| Jared voice | 10 |
| Truth-to-value translation | 15 |
| Persuasion / convincingness | 15 |
| Specificity & proof | 10 |
| Clarity & economy | 5 |

**Tier 1 = 80–100.** Employer-facing positioning below 80 should be rewritten before release.

This score does not weaken the underlying claim registry. Factual authority remains governed by `00-positioning/claims/`.

## Positioning pipeline

Evidence → hard factual boundary → evaluator context → strongest relevant supported truth → employer value → senior candidate voice → adversarial QA → release.

Not:

Evidence → inventory every unsupported inference → foreground caveats → cautiously describe what remains.

## Release rules

1. Lead with capability, scale, result, judgment, or employer value.
2. Keep caveats subordinate unless the distinction is material to the evaluator's question.
3. Never answer an objection the evaluator has not raised.
4. Do not confuse epistemic rigor with defensive candidate positioning.
5. Translate metrics into what they demonstrate without promoting them beyond their supported funnel level.
6. Prefer senior ownership language when the evidence supports ownership.
7. Preserve substantive detail. Do not improve positioning by making the work generic.
8. Avoid internal phrases such as "I do not overstate," "I am not positioning myself as," or "use only when" in employer-facing narrative.
9. Governance belongs underneath the story. It should increase trust without becoming the story.
10. Tailoring can change selection, emphasis, sequencing, and translation. It cannot change facts.

## Seniority test

A Tier 1 artifact should make clear not merely what Jared did, but what level of problem he can be trusted to own.

The recurring employer value proposition is the ability to connect growth strategy, capital allocation, performance media, CRO, measurement, agencies, operating systems, AI-enabled workflows, and executive decision-making across complex environments.

The intended impression is not "careful paid-media operator." It is "senior growth leader who can take a fragmented acquisition environment, make it legible, make the necessary decisions, and build a system that scales."
